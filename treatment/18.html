Exemplar of an Undeserving Ultra-Rich American (T18)

Now we ask you to carefully read the description on the next screen about a super billionaire tax evader and wealth inequality in the United States. Please imagine the description as vividly and with as many details as you can while reading the description:


“We don’t pay taxes. Only the little people pay taxes”

These are the words of New York hotel tycoon Leona Helmsley, who was listed among the 400 richest Americans. Helmsley was dubbed ‘The Queen of Mean’ for her tyrannical behavior towards employees who worked at her hotels and apartments in New York and across America. Even the smallest mistakes were used as grounds for firing, and employees often suffered insults and obscenities before being fired.

Helmsley, one of the wealthiest individuals in America, was convicted of tax evasion for using millions of company funds to lavishly renovate her mansion including an extravagant $1 million marble dance floor, a $210,000 mahogany card table, and a $130,000-stereo system. At the same time, Helmsley often refused to pay the ordinary workers building her house. She was also found guilty of conspiracy to defraud the United States, and dozens of other corruption charges. Despite this, Helmsley still had enough money to leave a $12 million trust fund for her aggressive Maltese dog that had a track record of biting employees and workers.

Was the super-billionaire Helmsley right when she claimed, ‘only the little people pay taxes?’ In 2018 the richest 400 households in America paid a lower effective tax-rate than the bottom 50% of ordinary Americans. Wealth inequality is growing in the United States and will soon be higher than any other developed country. It’s the ordinary hard-working people who shoulder the tax burden.

There is a solution: Increase taxes on the ultra-rich 0.1%.

An annual wealth tax on the richest 0.1% of U.S. households can make people like Helmsley contribute their fair share to society and help curb spiraling inequality. Ordinary hard-working Americans should not shoulder the tax burden alone. Hemsley’s statement ‘We don't pay taxes; only the little people pay taxes’ may have reflected her experience, but is that a fair deal? A simple tax on the ultra-rich 0.1% like Helmsley can help build a better America with opportunity for everyone and not just for the privileged few.
