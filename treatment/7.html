Highlighting the Uneven Effects of Wealth Taxes (T7)

Several governments around the world are considering measures to reduce wealth inequality, such as taxing the wealth of the top 0.1%. The introduction of such tax would affect different social groups in various ways. For instance, the additional tax revenue could be used to fund public education and transportation, benefiting individuals with lower wealth. Conversely, individuals with high wealth would need to contribute more financially. Now, we would like you to consider the potential consequences of such policies for these different social groups.

First, imagine yourself as a single parent raising two children. There is a high-quality private school in your hometown, but you cannot afford its tuition with your regular job. The local public school closed a year ago, forcing your children to travel an hour each day to the nearest public school, which is underfunded and does not offer the same level of education as the private school. Please answer the following open-ended questions:


In this situation, what would you be most stressed about?

How could the new tax revenue improve your life?


Now, imagine yourself as a person in the top 0.1% wealth bracket. You inherited a substantial amount of money but also worked hard to maximize your opportunities. With a wealth of $20,657,000, you are now subject to a 1% wealth tax, reducing your wealth to $20,450,430. Please answer the following open-ended questions:


In this situation, what would you be most stressed about?

How would the new tax affect your life negatively?
